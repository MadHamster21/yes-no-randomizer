# Proposed English Play Store listing

Prepared for the current repository build, version 3.0 (21). Confirm that the production release contains the photographed features before publishing this listing alongside it.

## App name

Yes/No Randomizer

Keep the recognizable name. An optional search-focused variant to test later is **Yes/No: Random Decision Maker** (28 characters).

## Short description

Roll a random yes or no, then float your answer while you multitask.

## Full description

Pizza tonight? One more episode? Take a walk?

Turn small everyday choices into a quick dice roll. Yes/No Randomizer gives you a random yes or no with a playful 3D animation. Type a question, or leave it blank, then tap Decide!

ONE TAP, TWO POSSIBILITIES
Watch the die roll and reveal your answer. Roll again whenever you want another random result.

FLOAT YOUR ANSWER
On devices that support picture-in-picture, tap Float answer after a roll to keep your answer in a floating window while you use another app. Tap the window to access New Answer and roll again without returning to the full screen.

MAKE YOURSELF AT HOME
Enjoy light and dark themes that follow your device settings. Choose from 18 language options inside the app, including English, Spanish, French, German, Arabic, Japanese, and more.

Your question and answer stay with you when you rotate your device or switch languages.

SIMPLE FROM THE START
No account, no ads, and no internet connection required. Open the app, ask a question if you like, and let chance choose.

Great for casual choices, friendly challenges, and those little "should I?" moments. Every result is random—just a fun nudge for everyday decisions.

## Why change the current listing?

- Lead with concrete, casual examples and explain the actual tap-and-roll experience.
- Replace generic promises about answering any question with an accurate explanation of randomness.
- Show the animated die, picture-in-picture, themes, and language selection; the current description does not explain them.
- Remove repeated calls to download and the awkward closing sentence.
- Use short, readable feature paragraphs rather than keyword repetition or a wall of emoji.

## Suggested experiment

Use the refreshed screenshot set with the proposed short description as the initial version. Once there is enough traffic for a meaningful comparison, test the first image's headline ("A little nudge. One tap." versus "Let the dice decide.") while keeping the rest unchanged. Do not infer a conversion lift from a small sample.
